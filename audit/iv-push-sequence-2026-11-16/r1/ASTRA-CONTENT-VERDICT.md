## Blocking finding 1 — C does not uniquely identify the initial check

Witness: `options[id=C].en` at candidate line 1087, and `.zh` at line 1088:

> In the medication preparation area, compare the medication vial label with the medication administration record.
>
> 在药物准备区，将药瓶标签与给药记录进行核对。

Location distinguishes the preparation room from the bedside. The vial object distinguishes the stock container from a newly labeled syringe. Neither distinguishes the initial check from a repeat check of that same vial in that same room. The stem explicitly presents selected steps; it does not state that the stock medication is still unverified. The rationales specify an initial check, but a post-answer rationale cannot add a missing premise to the question.

The clinical sources do **not** justify drawing up an unverified drug. They support both pre-withdrawal verification and repeated checks. The publisher's vial-preparation checklist places a label/MAR comparison before withdrawal, and permits another comparison after preparation before leaving for the patient. Open RN's parenteral checklist explicitly checks the vial, syringe and MAR after withdrawal. These sources establish that vial comparison is not intrinsically a pre-withdrawal-only action. They are general preparation references, not a claim that one institution's whole checklist universally governs IV push. [Taylor's checklist, steps 7–8 and 14–18](https://downloads.lww.com/wolterskluwer_vitalstream_com/sample-content/9781496384881_Lynn5e/samples/Skill05-4.pdf), [Open RN §18.8, preparation substeps 3–6](https://wtcs.pressbooks.pub/nursingskills/chapter/18-8-checklists-for-parenteral-medication-administration/).

**Checker inference:** A competent learner can assume the usual earlier verification, draw and immediately label the dose (A), repeat the vial/MAR check while still in the preparation room (C), then go to the bedside (B), complete omitted bedside safety checks, administer (E), and clear that dose from the line (D). That reading does not require administering or drawing an unverified drug. It also does not claim that every cited checklist labels the syringe before its second check: Open RN places its second check before labeling, within the composite A action. This independently exposes why the repeated check cannot be located uniquely just from its object; Taylor's policy-dependent post-preparation recheck supports the complete A,C alternative.

The source-backed distinction is between an initial check and a later recheck, not between safe and unsafe preparation. The candidate never tells the learner which occurrence C represents. No source proves that the word “vial” silently means the initial occurrence.

The independent enumeration finds:

- With the undisputed preparation-room/bedside, identity/administration, prepared-dose/administration and residual-dose/flush constraints: **A,C,B,E,D and C,A,B,E,D**.
- Adding the disputed C<A premise: **C,A,B,E,D** alone.

The producer proof is mechanically correct **conditional on C<A**. Its citation to ISMP §5.1 cannot turn that premise into a question-text fact: that section addresses clinical assessment and label/order verification around administration, not a rule that every preparation-room vial comparison denotes the first check. I therefore reject unique-key certification for this wording.

## Blocking finding 2 — rapid flush is categorically equated with excessive delivery

Witness: `rationale.correct.{en,zh}` (candidate lines 1105–1106) and `rationale.byChoice[refId=D].{en,zh}` (lines 1126–1127). The English says “because a rapid flush pushes the medication left in the line into the bloodstream too quickly” and “because flushing rapidly pushes the remaining medication into the bloodstream too quickly.” Chinese likewise says “因为快速冲管会使…药物过快进入血液”.

Both versions correctly call for a drug-appropriate rate and volume, but then state an unconditional causal conclusion about rapid flushing. A rapid flush is not inherently excessive for every IV push drug. The currently accessible adenosine product label explicitly directs a **“rapid saline flush”** after IV-line administration. That is a concrete counterexample within this item's unspecified IV push medication class, not a request to turn the item into an adenosine question. [DailyMed adenosine label, Dosage and Administration](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=d28ff4f3-baf4-4640-ae00-f0eb065417be).

The producer must make the risk conditional on exceeding the applicable drug-specific rate. The preceding drug/institution qualifier does not fully cure the following universal explanation in either language. The original commission itself requested a conditional risk statement. This is clinical meaning, not punctuation, so the checker cannot repair it mechanically and approve the result.
