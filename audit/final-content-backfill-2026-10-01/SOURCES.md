## FB-01

**Newborn transfer authorization.** NCMEC's current [infant-abduction resource page](https://www.missingkids.org/theissues/infantabductions) links [What Parents Need to Know](https://www.missingkids.org/content/dam/missingkids/pdfs/publications/expectantparentse.pdf), whose facility guidance distinguishes verified identification and transport authority. Its linked [professional guidelines, 10th edition](https://www.missingkids.org/content/dam/missingkids/pdfs/ForHealthCareProfessionals_10thEdition.pdf), printed pp. 17–18, discuss authorized transport personnel; the edition is dated 2014.

Use this authorization principle with an explicit facility process. Do not import a universal badge color, emergency code or identification technology from an older example. The eventual key remains to be reviewed.

## FB-02

**Hazardous-drug body fluids.** The [CDC/NIOSH resource hub](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/healthcare/hazardous-drugs/index.html), updated March 3, 2026, still directs readers to [Managing Hazardous Drug Exposures: Information for Healthcare Settings](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/127519/cdc_127519_DS1.pdf), published 2023. Printed p. 5 describes exposure through patient excreta and secretions. The [2024 hazardous-drug list and subsequent notices](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/publications/hcp/numbered/2025-103.html) help identify covered products; that list is not a complete task-control protocol.

Before authoring, source the named drug or explicitly established hazardous-drug status, task conditions and applicable protective controls. Do not assume a universal post-treatment interval or generalize infection PPE into chemical protection.

## FB-03

**Low-vision physical independence.** [NEI's low-vision guidance](https://www.nei.nih.gov/eye-health-information/eye-conditions-and-diseases/low-vision) establishes everyday functional limitations and adaptation as a relevant care concern. It does not, by itself, establish a specific bedside clock-face or orientation sequence.

Find direct support for the selected technique and demonstrate the client's independent use. Source status remains incomplete for an answer key.

## FB-04

**Dressing assistance.** [East Sussex Healthcare NHS Trust's occupational-therapy leaflet](https://www.esht.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2017/06/0413.pdf), page 1, provides an affected-arm-first dressing technique. The leaflet is dated October 2023, applies to injury/surgery or immobilization, and lists an October 2025 review date.

This is a useful lead, not sufficient grounds to call an adult stroke scenario source-ready. Obtain an appropriate current source or match the scenario to supported conditions. Avoid an unsupported unique multi-step ordering task.
