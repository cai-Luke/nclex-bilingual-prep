### 0.1 What step 4 is for, and what it cannot rely on

Amendment 1 Clause B §2.2 ultimately requires four proofs across a rebinding. Step 2 established the occurrence population. **This pre-ratification step 4 proves the current candidate's bijection over that population and establishes the fixed-family baseline. Clause B proofs 3 and 4 are comparative across an actual rebinding and are not discharged by a single-state candidate.** If a later rebinding occurs, its date-only diff and regenerated derived report must establish that only dependent surfaces changed and that every fixed surface remained byte-identical. This deliverable must not report either comparative proof as already passed. Two cautions bind the design.

First, step 4 validates the architect's mapping **against the step-2 census**, not against a fresh scan. If the mapping were validated only against itself, a span dropped between census and mapping would be invisible. §2.1 therefore re-anchors every mapping row to a census row *and* re-reads the bytes from the manifest.

Second, `M7.5` step 6 re-derives and proves the embedded report equals the regenerated report — but that is an equality between two outputs of the same generator. It cannot discover an error this step commits and then reproduces. §4's generation rules are therefore stated exactly, and §5 requires the generator to be driven from the validated mapping rather than from a re-derivation.

## 2. Validation

### 2.1 Anchor every mapping row to the census and to the bytes

For each of the 288 mapping rows, assert all of the following. Each failure is a **BLOCKER** reported with the offending `occ`, not corrected.

- the `occ` value appears exactly once in the mapping;
- a census row exists with the same `occ`, and its `start`, `end`, `line`, `col`, `literal`, `section`, `record_item` are byte-identical to the mapping row's carried values — **with the single exception at §2.2**;
- re-reading manifest bytes `[start, end)` returns exactly the row's `literal`;
- `end == start + 10`;
- the mapping's `context` equals the complete physical line containing `start`, read from the manifest.

State the row count each assertion covered. **Sampling is not permitted.**

### 2.2 The seven-row `section` exception

Occurrences `1` through `7` carry `section=M0` in both the census and the mapping. That value is not derivable — those spans sit at physical lines 13–16, in the manifest front matter, and `## M0. Header pins` does not begin until line 26. This was adjudicated at census acceptance as a post-execution application of **standing ruling 39**, and the mapping §2 carries it forward.

**Do not validate those seven `section` cells against any heading derivation, and do not report their non-derivability as a finding.** Validate every other field on those seven rows exactly as §2.1 requires. `section` is carried data on rows 1–7 and authoritative on rows 8–288.

This exception is closed-world: it covers occurrences 1–7 and no others. A `section` value that fails derivation on any row from 8 to 288 **is** a **BLOCKER**.

**Generated-report locator rule:** the known-bad carried value `M0` must not be embedded as the semantic section locator for occurrences 1–7. For those seven rows, emit the date-free locator value `front-matter`. For occurrences 8–288, emit the validated mapping `section` value unchanged. This is a presentation of the already-adjudicated seven-row exception, not a census repair or a reclassification.

### 2.3 Exclusivity and coverage

Assert mechanically, each as its own reported result:

1. every census `occ` from `1` to `288` appears in the mapping exactly once — no occurrence unmapped;
2. no `occ` appears twice — no occurrence multiply mapped;
3. every `surface_id` is unique across all 288 rows;
4. every `surface_id` is well-formed as `<family>.<n>`, where `<family>` is one of `D1`–`D6` or `F1`–`F11`, and `<n>` runs from `1` to the family's occurrence count with no gap and no repeat;
5. every row's `family` equals the family prefix of its own `surface_id`;
6. the per-family row counts sum to `288`.

Report the per-family counts you measure. **Do not compare them against any count stated in prose in the mapping, in Part D §8.1, or in any prior receipt** — those are family-level assertions and `M7.3` denies them authority. The row counts are the population.

### 2.4 Dependent-partition check

Report, as measured values and without adjudicating them:

- the count of occurrences whose `family` begins with `D`;
- the count whose `literal` equals `2026-08-18`;
- whether those two sets are the same set of `occ` values, and if not, the symmetric difference.

**A difference here is a finding returned to the architect seat, not a BLOCKER and not a repair.** A dependent surface currently rendering a non-bound literal, or a fixed surface coincidentally carrying the bound literal, are both possible in principle and both are the architect's to dispose.

### 2.5 Fixed-family baseline sanity check

For every row whose `family` begins with `F`, assert that its `literal` is not `2026-08-18`. Report the count checked and any exception with its `occ`. An exception is a **finding**, not a BLOCKER, and is returned rather than corrected.

**This is a current-state sanity check, not Clause B proof 4.** Byte-identity of fixed surfaces is a comparative proof across a rebinding and cannot be established from this single candidate state. Whether a family's stated fixedness ground is correct is a semantic question reserved to the architect seat and to the commission review. ## 4. Report generation

Generate the derived report bytes from the **validated mapping**, not from a re-derivation and not from the census.

`M7.5` fixes the content rule: report rows carry occurrence IDs, family IDs, concrete semantic locators, byte spans, and dependent/fixed dispositions — and **no literal dates, no archive filenames, and no quoted date-bearing snippets**, so that the report cannot become its own occurrence population.

That rule is absolute and is the sharpest constraint in this order. Concretely, for every generated byte:

- **no `[0-9]{4}-[0-9]{2}-[0-9]{2}` token may appear anywhere in the generated bytes**, including inside a family ground, a locator, a section label, or a heading;
- do not emit the `literal` column;
- do not emit the `context` column, in whole or in excerpt;
- do not emit any filename, path, or anchor slug that contains a date;
- for this report, the concrete semantic locator is the tuple `section_locator`, `record_item`, and `surface_id`; `section_locator` is `front-matter` for occurrences 1–7 and the validated mapping `section` for occurrences 8–288. The byte span is carried separately as `start` and `end`. Generate no free-text locator description.

**After generating, scan the generated bytes with the step-2 occurrence rule and all four step-2 §2.4 near-miss scans, and assert zero hits on all five scans.** Report each measured count. Any non-zero count is a **BLOCKER** and the generated bytes are not returned.

Structure the report as: a header stating the population and the family register with per-family counts; then one row per occurrence in ascending `occ` order, carrying `occ`, `surface_id`, `family`, disposition (`dependent` / `fixed`), `section_locator`, `record_item`, `start`, `end`.

Do not author prose rationale, do not restate the family grounds, and do not draft any sentence that adjudicates a family assignment. Do not emit a generation date, timestamp, month-name date, slash date, dot date, or other date-formatted prose in the generated block; its header and family register must be date-free.
